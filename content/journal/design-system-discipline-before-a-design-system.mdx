Most projects that would benefit from a design system will never get one — not a
real, documented, versioned one with a dedicated owner. They're too small, too early,
or too fast-moving for that kind of investment. And that's fine. The value of a design
system was never really the Figma library. It was the discipline behind it.

You can have most of that discipline with none of the tooling.

## Three decisions, made once

The vast majority of visual inconsistency in a small project comes down to the same
three things being re-decided, slightly differently, every single time someone builds
a new screen:

- **Spacing.** Is the gap between a heading and a paragraph 12px this time, or 16px?
- **Color.** Is this gray `#71717a` or `#737373` or `#6b6b72`? Nobody remembers, so
  everyone eyeballs it.
- **Type scale.** Is a card title `text-lg` or `text-xl`? Depends who built the card.

None of these require a design system to solve. They require a single source of truth
that's slightly more annoying to bypass than to use correctly.

```css
:root {
  --space-2: 0.5rem;
  --space-4: 1rem;
  --space-6: 1.5rem;
  --space-8: 2rem;

  --color-fg-muted: #a1a1aa;
  --color-fg-subtle: #6b6b72;
}
```

That's it. That's most of a design system's actual value, expressed in a dozen lines.
The rest — the documentation site, the Figma components, the versioned package — is
infrastructure for a _team_ to stay in sync. If it's just you, or a small team shipping
fast, the tokens are the discipline. The tooling is optional.

## Constraints are the feature, not the limitation

The instinct when something feels limiting is to add an escape hatch — "just this once,
I'll use `18px` instead of a token." Every one of those exceptions is a tiny withdrawal
from the same account: the reason the site feels considered instead of assembled.

> A design system isn't a set of components. It's a small number of decisions you've
> agreed not to keep re-litigating.

I'd rather work inside four spacing values and two type scales I trust completely than
have unlimited freedom I have to re-justify on every screen. Constraints, chosen well
early, are what make a one-person project look like it came out of a much larger,
more disciplined studio.

## Where to actually draw the line

Start with tokens for color, spacing, and type — nothing else. Resist the urge to
formalize component variants until you've built the same pattern three times by hand
and felt the friction yourself. A design system built ahead of real, repeated need is
just speculative architecture wearing a nicer outfit.
